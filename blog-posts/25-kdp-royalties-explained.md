## 1. Amazon KDP eBook Royalties: The 70% vs. 35% Rule

When publishing a digital Kindle edition, Amazon offers authors a choice between two distinct **kdp royalty rates**: the **70% royalty option** and the **35% royalty option**. Choosing the right tier requires understanding Amazon's strict pricing criteria.

### The 70% Royalty Option (The Sweet Spot)
To qualify for Amazon's coveted 70% royalty rate, your eBook must satisfy three non-negotiable requirements:
1. **Price Bracket**: Your retail list price must be set strictly between **$2.99 and $9.99 (USD)**.
2. **Discount Requirement**: Your list price must be at least 20% lower than the physical print edition of the same title.
3. **Territory Eligibility**: The customer must reside within designated 70% territories (including the US, UK, Canada, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Australia, Japan, Brazil, Mexico, and India).

### The Hidden Deduction: Amazon Delivery Fees
Under the 70% royalty tier, Amazon deducts a mandatory "digital delivery fee" based on your eBook's final file size. In the US marketplace, this fee is **$0.15 per megabyte (MB)**:
- A text-heavy novel with a tiny file size of **0.8 MB** incurs a delivery fee of approximately **$0.12**.
- An illustrated non-fiction book or children's book with unoptimized images totaling **12 MB** incurs a crushing delivery fee of **$1.80** on every single download!

$$\text{Net 70% Royalty} = (\text{List Price} \times 0.70) - \text{Delivery Fee}$$

*Example*: Selling a 1.0 MB eBook at $4.99 yields:
$$\text{Royalty} = ($4.99 \times 0.70) - $0.15 = $3.49 - $0.15 = \mathbf{$3.34\text{ per copy}}$$

### The 35% Royalty Option (When Does It Apply?)
Amazon defaults to the 35% royalty plan under specific commercial circumstances:
- If your eBook is priced **below $2.99** (such as promotional $0.99 loss-leaders).
- If your eBook is priced **above $9.99** (such as premium textbooks or multi-book omnibuses).
- For sales made to customers in countries outside the 70% territory list.
- **The Delivery Fee Advantage**: The 35% royalty plan charges **zero delivery fees**, regardless of how large your file size is. For image-heavy graphic books over 20 MB, the 35% plan can occasionally yield higher net profits than the 70% plan.

## 2. KDP Print Royalties: Paperbacks and Hardcovers

Unlike eBooks (which have near-zero marginal production costs), physical books require ink, paper, glue, and shipping logistics. Amazon pays a **60% gross royalty rate** for standard paperback distribution, from which fixed and per-page print costs are deducted.

### The Amazon Royalty Calculator Formula for Print:
$$\text{Print Royalty} = (\text{List Price} \times 60\%) - \text{Printing Cost}$$

### Calculating KDP Printing Costs (US Market):
Amazon uses fixed base formulas to calculate physical manufacturing costs:
- **Black & White Interior (under 108 pages)**: Flat rate of **$2.30** (no per-page cost).
- **Black & White Interior (110 to 828 pages)**: **$1.00 fixed cost + ($0.013 x page count)**.
- **Standard Color Interior (over 40 pages)**: **$1.00 fixed cost + ($0.036 x page count)**.
- **Premium Color Interior (over 24 pages)**: **$1.00 fixed cost + ($0.075 x page count)**.

### Real-World Mathematical Example:
Consider a standard 320-page black-and-white novel formatted in a 5.5" x 8.5" trim size, retailing at **$14.99**:
1. **Printing Cost**: $1.00 + (320 x $0.013) = $1.00 + $4.16 = **$5.16**.
2. **Amazon 60% Gross Share**: $14.99 x 0.60 = **$8.99**.
3. **Author Net Royalty Per Sale**: $8.99 - $5.16 = **$3.83**.

Selling 1,000 copies of this paperback generates **$3,830.00** in net print royalties.

### Expanded Distribution Royalties (40% Tier)
If you enable "Expanded Distribution" to make your paperback orderable by libraries and non-Amazon retailers, Amazon's gross royalty rate drops from 60% to **40%**:
$$\text{Expanded Royalty} = (\text{List Price} \times 40\%) - \text{Printing Cost}$$
On that same $14.99 book: ($14.99 x 0.40) - $5.16 = **$0.84 net royalty**. While the per-unit payout is lower, it captures sales from outside the Amazon ecosystem with zero extra effort.

## 3. Kindle Unlimited (KU) and KENP Page Read Payouts

If you enroll your eBook in KDP Select, you unlock Amazon's massive subscription audience: Kindle Unlimited (KU). KU subscribers do not buy your book; they borrow it for free, and Amazon compensates you from the monthly **KDP Select Global Fund** (which regularly exceeds $50 million per month).

### How KENP Works:
Amazon calculates payouts based on **Kindle Edition Normalized Pages (KENP)** read by subscribers:
- KENP is a normalized measurement that standardizes font size, margins, and line spacing across all books to ensure fair compensation.
- The average KENP payout rate fluctuates between **$0.0040 and $0.0046 per page read**.
- A reader who finishes a 300-page fantasy novel earns the author approximately **$1.26 to $1.38**.
- If 2,000 KU subscribers read your 300-page series in a month, you generate approximately **$2,640.00** purely from page reads, completely separate from retail purchases.

## 4. Payment Schedules and Logistics: How KDP Pays Authors

Understanding Amazon's payment pipeline ensures smooth cash flow management for your author business:

### The 60-Day Payment Lag
Amazon operates on a **net-60 payment schedule**. Royalties earned during a specific calendar month are paid out approximately 60 days later:
- Royalties earned in **January** are paid at the end of **March**.
- Royalties earned in **February** are paid at the end of **April**.
- Royalties earned in **March** are paid at the end of **May**.

### Electronic Funds Transfer (Direct Deposit)
In the United States, United Kingdom, Eurozone, Canada, Australia, and dozens of other territories, Amazon delivers royalties via Direct Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT). There is **no minimum payment threshold** for EFT payments—even if you earn $3.45 in a month, Amazon will deposit it directly into your bank account.

### Tax Withholding Rules:
- **US Citizens**: Amazon reports earnings annually on IRS Form 1099-MISC (or 1099-NEC) with zero tax withheld, provided a valid SSN or EIN is registered in your tax interview.
- **International Authors**: By default, the US IRS mandates a 30% statutory withholding tax on royalties earned from US sales. However, if your country maintains a bilateral tax treaty with the United States (such as the UK, Canada, Australia, or Pakistan), entering your local foreign tax ID in KDP's automated tax interview reduces your US withholding tax to **0% to 10%**.

## Frequently Asked Questions (PAA)

### How much does KDP take from book sales?
On eBooks priced between $2.99 and $9.99, Amazon takes 30% of the list price (plus a small data delivery fee of $0.15/MB), leaving the author with 70%. On paperbacks, Amazon takes 40% of the list price plus the physical print manufacturing cost, paying the author the remaining 60% gross balance.

### When does Amazon KDP pay royalties?
Amazon KDP pays royalties on a monthly schedule, approximately 60 days following the end of the calendar month in which sales occurred. For example, royalties earned in January are deposited into your bank account at the end of March.

### Is Kindle Unlimited worth it for indie authors?
Yes, Kindle Unlimited is exceptionally lucrative for fiction authors in genres like romance, fantasy, science fiction, and thrillers, where avid readers consume multiple books weekly. KENP page-read payouts frequently account for 40% to 70% of a fiction author's total monthly royalties.
