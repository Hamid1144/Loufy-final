# How to Add Page Numbers and Headers to a Book: The Professional Pagination Guide (2026)

Pick up any bestselling novel published by Penguin Random House, Macmillan, or HarperCollins. Open to chapter three. Notice the quiet, disciplined architecture framing the top and bottom of each page: on the left-hand page, the author's name sits in refined small capitals; on the right-hand page, the book's title or current chapter name guides the eye. Notice also that the opening page of each chapter is clean and unburdened—free of distracting top headers—and that the front matter uses lowercase Roman numerals while the story begins cleanly on page one. For debut self-published authors, replicating this traditional publishing standard can feel maddening. In this comprehensive **book pagination guide**, you will learn exactly **how to add page numbers and headers to a book** without tearing your hair out over shifting section links.

Whether you are configuring your files in Adobe InDesign, formatting in Microsoft Word, or utilizing tools like Atticus, this tutorial breaks down the ironclad rules of **page numbering for print books** and commercial running headers.

## 1. The Golden Rules of Book Pagination: Recto, Verso, and Folios

Before clicking any software menus, you must master the fundamental vocabulary and structural geometry of book design:

### Recto vs. Verso Pages
- **Recto (Right-Hand Page)**: Always bears an **odd page number** (1, 3, 5, 7...). In traditional bookbinding, major sections—including the Half Title, Title Page, Dedication, Table of Contents, and Chapter 1—should ideally open on a recto (right-hand) page.
- **Verso (Left-Hand Page)**: Always bears an **even page number** (2, 4, 6, 8...). Verso pages house the Copyright page, dedication overflows, and continuous narrative body spreads.

### The Rule of the "Blind Folio"
In professional typography, a **folio** is simply a page number. A **blind folio** refers to a page that technically counts toward the book's total page calculation but does not visually display a printed page number. 

You must strictly suppress visible page numbers on:
1. The Half Title and Title pages.
2. The Copyright page.
3. The Dedication and Epigraph pages.
4. Any completely blank pages inserted to force a chapter to open on the right-hand page.
5. The **opening page of every single chapter**.

Printing a visible page number or running header across the top of a chapter title page is the #1 telltale sign of an amateur self-published book.

## 2. Standard Industry Conventions for Running Headers

When configuring **running headers book design**, commercial publishers adhere to strict structural pairings:

| Running Header Element | Standard Placement | Typography Style |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Author Name** | Verso (Left-hand / Even page) | Centered or left-aligned; 8.5 pt to 9.5 pt; Small Caps; letter-spacing +100 |
| **Book Title (or Chapter Title)**| Recto (Right-hand / Odd page) | Centered or right-aligned; 8.5 pt to 9.5 pt; Small Caps or Italics; letter-spacing +100 |
| **Page Number (Top Folio)** | Outer margins of both Verso and Recto headers | Set at the same font size and baseline as the running header text |
| **Page Number (Bottom Folio)**| Centered in footer across both pages | Clean, subtle 9 pt font; leaves top header purely for titles |

### Top Folios vs. Bottom Drop Folios
- **Top Folio**: The page number is integrated directly into the outer edge of the running header. On the first page of a chapter (where the header is suppressed), the page number is either omitted completely or moved to the bottom center (called a "drop folio").
- **Bottom Centered Folio**: The page number sits centered in the footer on every narrative page, while the header is reserved exclusively for the author and book title. This is the simplest and cleanest layout for indie authors.

## 3. Step-by-Step: Adding Page Numbers and Headers in Microsoft Word

Formatting headers and page numbers in Word requires breaking your manuscript into isolated, independent sections.

### Step 1: Insert Section Breaks (Next Page)
Standard page breaks (Ctrl + Enter) maintain header continuity. To control headers independently, you must use Section Breaks:
- Place your cursor at the very end of your front matter (e.g., after the Table of Contents), go to **Layout > Breaks**, and select **Section Break (Next Page)**.
- Repeat this step at the end of every chapter throughout your entire manuscript.

### Step 2: Unlink Headers from Previous Sections
By default, Word automatically checks "Link to Previous" on all new sections. You must sever this digital tether:
1. Double-click the header area on the first page of Section 2 (Chapter 1).
2. On the **Header & Footer** ribbon tab, click the **Link to Previous** button to toggle it OFF.
3. Switch down to the footer area and click **Link to Previous** to turn it OFF in the footer as well.

### Step 3: Configure "Different Odd & Even Pages" and "Different First Page"
1. In the Header & Footer ribbon, check the box for **Different Odd & Even Pages**. This enables you to enter the Author Name on even pages and the Book Title on odd pages.
2. Check the box for **Different First Page**. This automatically suppresses the running header on the first page of your chapter.

### Step 4: Configure Number Formatting
1. In the front matter section, click **Page Number > Format Page Numbers**, select lowercase Roman numerals (i, ii, iii), and start at 'i'.
2. In Chapter 1, click **Page Number > Format Page Numbers**, select standard Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3), and select **Start at: 1**.

## 4. Setting Up Headers and Page Numbers in Adobe InDesign

For authors and formatters utilizing Adobe InDesign, pagination is controlled via **Parent Pages (Master Pages)**:

### 1. Creating Parent Page Spreads
1. Open the **Pages** panel and navigate to the **A-Parent** spread.
2. Draw a text frame across the top of the left-hand (verso) page. Type your Author Name.
3. Draw a text frame across the top of the right-hand (recto) page. Insert the dynamic chapter variable via **Type > Text Variables > Insert Variable > Chapter Number/Name** or type your Book Title.

### 2. Inserting Dynamic Page Number Markers
To insert an automatic page number in InDesign:
- Position your cursor where you want the folio to appear.
- Go to **Type > Insert Special Character > Markers > Current Page Number** (or press **Ctrl + Alt + Shift + N**).
- On the A-Parent page, this displays as the letter "A". On your actual document pages, it automatically calculates the correct sequential number.

### 3. Suppressing Headers on Chapter Openers
Create a secondary **B-Parent** (or "None") with no headers. Simply drag the blank parent page onto the opening page of each chapter in the Pages panel to instantly hide the running header.

## 5. Automated Pagination in Vellum and Atticus

If you are using dedicated indie formatting software like Vellum or Atticus, pagination and running headers are handled automatically behind the scenes:

- Both tools enforce different odd and even page spreads.

## Frequently Asked Questions (PAA)

### What is the rule for running headers in a book?
The industry standard convention is to place the Author Name on the verso (left-hand, even-numbered) page header, and the Book Title or Chapter Name on the recto (right-hand, odd-numbered) page header. Running headers must always be suppressed on chapter opening pages.

### Do page numbers go on the left or right of a book?
In a printed book, odd page numbers always sit on the right-hand (recto) page, and even page numbers always sit on the left-hand (verso) page. Page numbers can be positioned at the outer top corners, outer bottom corners, or centered in the bottom footer.

### Why do page numbers disappear or duplicate in Word?
This happens when Section Breaks are improperly configured or when "Link to Previous" remains activated across sections. To fix it, ensure you use Section Breaks (Next Page) between chapters, unlink headers from the preceding section, and verify that "Different First Page" is enabled.
